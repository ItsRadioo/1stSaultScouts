Welcome to the Crew

Rovers plan their Crew's experiences, build practical skills and contribute to local Scouting. Members share responsibility for the activities, communications and decisions that make the Crew successful.

Regular meeting: Meetings arranged at the Crew's discretion.

Regular location: St. Paul’s Presbyterian Church, 136 Cathcart Street, Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, with outdoor meetings at other locations as communicated by Scouters.

What a meeting might look like

Arrive, greet your fellow members, take part in opening activities, work on a game, project or adventure, reflect on what you learned and help put equipment away. Outdoor meetings may replace the usual church meeting.

Have a water bottle and weather-appropriate clothing. Ask the leaders about any equipment needed for the next adventure.

Chapter 1

Investiture and joining

Investiture is a welcome into the section and a chance to make or renew your Scouting commitment. You can prepare with these activities and your Scouters will explain the ceremony.

Traditions to learn

  • Agree on the Crew's expectations, traditions and membership commitments.
  • Review the Promise, Law and any investiture or welcoming process used by the Crew.
  • Understand your responsibilities as an adult member, particularly when working with younger sections.

Chapter 2

Your uniform and insignia

The uniform identifies your section, celebrates achievement and connects you with the wider Scouting community. Confirm what to purchase with the group before buying new items.

Uniform pieces to discuss with your Scouter

  • Rover uniform or identifiers as directed by the Crew and current Scouts Canada guidance
  • Group neckerchief, woggle and approved insignia where applicable
  • Activity-specific clothing, personal safety equipment and appropriate footwear
  • The current official insignia chart for any badges

See official badge placement diagrams

Check the section-specific Scouts Canada chart before sewing insignia. Temporary event crests are not necessarily worn in the same places as earned badges. Your Scouter can help with placement.

Care for your uniform

  • Label your clothing and keep your neckerchief and woggle together.
  • Check badges and seams before important ceremonies.
  • Wear weather-safe layers and suitable footwear for the activity, even when you wear your uniform.

Chapter 4

Skills, games and adventure ideas

Your section mixes hands-on challenges, outdoor exploration, community service and leadership. The examples below are possibilities, not a fixed meeting calendar.

  • Organize expeditions, route plans, budgets, equipment and communications.
  • Mentor Scouting youth appropriately while meeting safeguarding and screening requirements.
  • Practise emergency planning, environmental responsibility and responsible leadership.
  • Review project outcomes and improve how the Crew operates.

Try the planning cycle

  1. Plan: What would you like to try? What help or equipment do you need?
  2. Do: Participate safely, practise your skills and help your team.
  3. Review: What worked? What would you change next time?

For a project at home, always check with an adult before using tools, fires, chemicals or outdoor equipment.

Chapter 5

Camping, sleepovers and packing

Every camp is different. A church sleepover, a cabin camp, a summer tent camp and a winter trip require different gear. The event notice and leaders' instructions always take priority.

  • Develop detailed kit lists matched to the activity and the capabilities of the participants.
  • Assign and inspect shared equipment and document activity arrangements.
  • Include contingency plans for weather, delayed travel and equipment failure.

Basic overnight checklist

Before you leave

  • Check the forecast and the specific gear list from your leaders.
  • Ask about food, allergies, permitted snacks, sleeping arrangements and pickup details.
  • Keep personal medication information private and provide it through the group's approved process.
  • Do not bring knives, axes, matches or other specialized gear unless leaders specifically authorize and supervise it.

Chapter 6

Beyond Rovers

Moving up is a new adventure, not starting from zero. Your friends and leaders can help you prepare, and skills you already developed remain valuable.

  • Consider becoming a screened volunteer Scouter or supporting the Group Committee.
  • Carry your leadership, service and outdoor skills into community projects.
  • Ask the Group Commissioners about current volunteer registration, training and screening.
