Getting started

Welcome to 1st Sault Ste. Marie

Our Scout Group brings young people together for outdoor adventures, friendship, practical skills, leadership and community involvement. This guide is a starting point for families, not a replacement for trip notices or Scouts Canada policies.

Where we meet

Regular location: St. Paul’s Presbyterian Church, 136 Cathcart Street, Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario. Some meetings and activities take place at various outdoor locations; the section will communicate those arrangements.

Ages 5–7 · Mondays, 6:30–7:45 p.m.

Beaver Scouts

Discovering the world through play, curiosity and friendship.

What to expect

Beaver Scouts is an introduction to adventure. Scouters guide short, active experiences where every child has a chance to participate, practise and feel part of the Colony.

Activities and skills

  • Nature scavenger hunts, animal tracks, exploring seasons and caring for the outdoors
  • Crafts, simple science experiments, stories, games and themed adventures
  • Short hikes, safe campfires, introductory knots and simple emergency awareness
  • Kindness and community activities, team challenges and personal achievement badges

At meetings

  • Bring a reusable water bottle, indoor shoes where requested and clothing suitable for the weather.
  • Practise staying with the group, asking for help and respecting other people's space and belongings.
  • Parents help with packing and labelled clothing; Beavers learn gradually to manage their belongings.

Camps and overnights

Overnights can be indoor sleepovers or outdoor camps, depending on the event. Families should pack simple, labelled items that the Beaver can identify independently.

Personal packing suggestions

  • Sleeping bag appropriate to the venue and season
  • Sleeping mat or approved sleeping arrangement
  • Pillow and comfortable sleepwear
  • Complete change of clothes, two extra pairs of socks and underwear
  • Weather-appropriate jacket, warm layers, hat and suitable footwear
  • Toothbrush, toothpaste, small towel and personal hygiene supplies
  • Water bottle, flashlight or headlamp with batteries
  • Comfort item if permitted by the event leaders

Planning tip: Pack together, let your Beaver carry their own day bag, and put wet-weather gear where they can find it quickly.

Ages 8–10 · Wednesdays, starting at 6:30 p.m.

Cub Scouts

Building independence through teamwork and hands-on challenges.

What to expect

Cubs take on longer adventures and increasingly make choices about their program. Pack activities encourage cooperation, creative problem-solving and practical outdoor confidence.

Activities and skills

  • Trail hikes, map games, nature exploration and outdoor observation
  • Knots, simple shelter building, safe cooking introductions and fire safety
  • Team challenges, building projects, service and community events
  • Badges, Outdoor Adventure Skills and planning portions of their adventures

At meetings

  • Arrive ready to participate, listen to safety instructions and contribute to team activities.
  • Learn to pack and organize a personal day bag and care for borrowed equipment.
  • Participate respectfully in group decisions and practise solving disagreements.

Camps and overnights

Cubs may attend indoor overnights, cabin camps and tent camps. The event notice will identify which sleeping system and seasonal equipment are required.

Personal packing suggestions

  • Season-appropriate sleeping bag and sleeping mat
  • Pillow and sleepwear
  • Clothes for every day plus spare socks and underwear
  • Warm layers and waterproof outerwear
  • Sturdy shoes or boots and optional camp shoes if instructed
  • Toiletries, small towel and any required personal items
  • Water bottle, headlamp or flashlight
  • Daypack and labelled bag for dirty or wet clothes

Planning tip: Have your Cub lay everything out, check each item, then pack their bag themselves with an adult reviewing the result.

Ages 11–14 · Wednesdays, starting at 6:30 p.m.

Scouts

Youth-led adventures, patrol responsibility and outdoor skills.

What to expect

Scouts become increasingly involved in planning their own activities and camps. Patrols organize work, practise leadership and learn to assess the conditions and equipment needed for safe adventures.

Activities and skills

  • Map and compass navigation, route planning, hiking and trail stewardship
  • Camp cooking, firecraft, shelter building and supervised tool skills
  • Patrol-led games, outdoor challenges and community service
  • Developing Outdoor Adventure Skills, assessing risks and practising Leave No Trace

At meetings

  • Take responsibility for personal kit, attendance information and shared tasks.
  • Participate in patrol planning, follow activity safety boundaries and respect equipment.
  • Build resilience through preparation, teamwork and reflection after activities.

Camps and overnights

Weekend camps and multi-day activities may involve tent camping or hiking. The patrol plan and Scouters will specify shared equipment, menus and any backpacking requirements.

Personal packing suggestions

  • Appropriate sleeping bag and insulated sleeping mat
  • Tent or shared shelter only if assigned by leaders
  • Layered clothing, waterproof jacket and extra socks
  • Suitable boots or shoes for the planned terrain
  • Water bottle and personal eating kit if requested
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • Toiletries, hand towel and labelled medications handled per event instructions
  • Backpack appropriate to the activity and waterproof dry bags or liners

Planning tip: Use the actual weather forecast and trip plan. Scouts should inspect their own kit for fit, function and waterproofing before departure.

Ages 15–17 · One Thursday per month; dates and start times shared by the Company.

Venturer Scouts

Ambitious activities planned and shaped by youth.

What to expect

Venturers set goals, manage more complex projects and contribute leadership in their Company. Activities reflect the interests and experience of participants and the safety standards for each adventure.

Activities and skills

  • Planning and delivering expeditions, campouts and outdoor challenges
  • Advanced navigation, paddling, campcraft and practical leadership where properly supported
  • Community service, teamwork and personal development projects
  • Mentoring younger sections where appropriate and approved

At meetings

  • Contribute actively to planning, budgeting and communication.
  • Be prepared with the skills and equipment called for by each activity.
  • Work with Scouters and fellow Venturers to assess risk and meet agreed responsibilities.

Camps and overnights

Equipment requirements vary greatly between cabin weekends, hikes, paddling trips and winter activities. The trip-specific equipment list always takes precedence.

Personal packing suggestions

  • Season-rated sleep system and shelter arrangement as directed
  • Suitable layered clothing, rainwear and footwear
  • Appropriate backpack or dry bag for the travel method
  • Navigation equipment and personal eating kit if directed
  • Water bottle and other hydration equipment as required
  • Headlamp and spare power
  • Toiletries and personal care items
  • Activity-specific safety gear only as directed by the trip organizers

Planning tip: Venturers should review itinerary, forecasts, shared gear assignments and emergency arrangements before each departure.

Ages 18–26 · Meetings organized at the Crew's discretion.

Rover Scouts

Self-directed adventure, service and leadership.

What to expect

Rovers shape their own Crew program through shared interests, personal goals, service and adventure. Members organize activities and bring increasing responsibility to planning and participation.

Activities and skills

  • Outdoor expeditions and technical skills development
  • Community service and volunteer leadership
  • Travel, camping and crew-designed challenges
  • Mentoring and supporting the wider Scouting community

At meetings

  • Agree on plans, budgets and communications with the Crew.
  • Arrive equipped and appropriately prepared for the activity.
  • Take responsibility for shared equipment, decision-making and safe participation.

Camps and overnights

A Rover activity may range from a day trip to a longer expedition. Participants set an activity-specific kit list rather than relying on one standard weekend list.

Personal packing suggestions

  • Sleep system matched to the season and accommodation
  • Clothing layers, waterproof protection and suitable footwear
  • Water and food equipment as required by the Crew plan
  • Headlamp, personal care kit and personal necessities
  • Trip-specific navigation and communication equipment
  • Appropriate backpack, dry bags or storage system
  • Any specialized equipment assigned for the activity

Planning tip: Create a shared kit inventory and distinguish communal equipment from items every Rover must carry.

Prepare for adventure

Basic camp packing checklist

This is a general personal kit list for a typical overnight, not a universal requirement. Gear varies by season, indoor or outdoor sleeping, trip duration and activities. Your section's event-specific instructions always take priority.

Before leaving home

  • Label belongings clearly, especially bottles, jackets and flashlights.
  • Pack spare clothing where the youth can easily find it, preferably in separate labelled bags.
  • Check the weather and plan for rain, wind, cold evenings and temperature changes.
  • Avoid valuables, electronics, tools, knives and food unless specifically requested or permitted.
  • Ask leaders about accommodations, allergies, medication handling and any missing gear.
  • Confirm arrival and pickup location, timing, emergency contact arrangements and who is authorized to collect the youth.

Winter or cold-weather additions

  • Extra insulated layers, warm socks, mittens and a warm hat
  • Water-resistant or insulated boots
  • An appropriately rated sleeping system for the venue and forecast
  • Spare dry gloves and a waterproof bag for wet gear

Gear questions? You do not need to purchase expensive equipment before speaking with your section leaders. Ask what is needed for the actual activity.

For parents and guardians

Helping your youth get the most from Scouting

Registration and uniforms

Registration is managed through MyScouts. Visit our Uniforms and badges page to view official insignia placement charts. Ask the group which items are required for your section before purchasing.

Safety and inclusion

Tell the section leadership team privately about information needed to support safe participation, including allergies, access needs and medication arrangements. Activities follow the applicable Scouts Canada standards and the directions in each event notice.

Outdoor meetings

Outdoor activities are a core part of our program. Dress for the weather, bring water and suitable footwear, and watch section communications for any alternate meeting point.

Participation and communication

Notify your section if your youth will be absent when advance notice is requested. Encourage youth to speak up if they are uncomfortable or need help. If you have a concern, start with your section leaders or reach out to the Group Commissioners.
